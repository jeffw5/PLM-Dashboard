01

What the RE Gateway is

The Requirements Engineering (RE) Gateway is the checkpoint every AI-assisted interaction passes through before it's allowed to act, and again before its answer is allowed to reach anyone. It sits between "someone asked the AI to do something" and "the AI did it" — and its only job is to make sure that whatever happens next is grounded in an actual, current, traceable business requirement, not just in what a model felt like generating.

In practice that means the Gateway resolves every request against a living rulebook — the organization's actual compliance, safety, ethical, and outcome requirements, stored as data rather than buried in a policy PDF — checks the AI's proposed response against that rulebook, and blocks or holds anything that fails, is unclear, or can't be undone without a person signing off on it first.

Think of it like…

An air-traffic control tower for AI-assisted work: nothing takes off without a filed flight plan, nothing lands without clearance, and anything irreversible gets a human controller's explicit go-ahead — while every decision along the way is logged, so if something goes wrong later, you can replay exactly why the tower cleared it.

02

How it works, end to end

Seven checkpoints, in order. Each one is enforced automatically, every time — no request skips the line, and nothing downstream can quietly bypass a step upstream missed.

📨
1The request is captured, structuredSPO

Who's asking, what outcome they actually need, which part of the business it touches, and the situation around it — captured as structured data, not a loose sentence the system has to guess the intent of.

🔍
2Every relevant rule is pulled, liveRCB

Before anything else happens, the Gateway assembles the complete, current set of requirements that govern this exact request — compliance rules, safety limits, scope boundaries, the outcomes that actually matter to this stakeholder.

🛡️
3Meaning and rules are checkedSHACL · SWRL

Terms are resolved against the organization's own definitions (so "customer," "incident," or "material change" mean one consistent thing), and the request is tested against every constraint that applies — silently, automatically, before a response is even drafted.

🔐
4Anything irreversible gets flagged earlyReversibility PEP

If what's being proposed can't be undone — sending money, deleting a record, notifying a regulator — that's decided before the AI ever attempts it, not discovered after the fact.

📋
5One governed instruction set is compiledCAD

Every check, flag, and rule from the steps above is compiled into a single directive the AI must operate under — what it's allowed to do, what it must watch for, and what it can't touch without approval.

✅
6The output is graded before it's shown to anyoneService Facade

A ten-point scorecard — did it address the actual outcome, cite what it should, stay in scope, respect the persona's access — has to pass. If it doesn't, the AI is sent back with specific, structured corrections. Repeated failures trigger a formal review automatically.

🔒
7Irreversible action waits for a named humanSimplex Gateway

For anything flagged in step 4, the system pauses. A specific, authorized person sees exactly what will happen and what can't be undone, and must approve it — with an identity, a timestamp, and an immutable record — before it executes.

👤
8A governed answer arrives, with its receiptsDelivery

The stakeholder gets the substantive answer and a plain view of what shaped it: which requirements applied, what passed validation, and — surfaced openly, not buried — anything that's still a gap.

03

Stakeholder needs, in a fixed order: Planet, People, Profit

The Gateway doesn't treat "the business case," "the people affected," and "the planet this all happens on" as three inputs to be balanced case by case. They're ranked, in this order, without exception — and every governance decision the pipeline makes is a live expression of that ranking, not just a value statement on a wall somewhere.

1
Planet
The shared, physical world everything else depends on. Enforced hardest at the points where an action can't be undone — nothing with irreversible planetary- or societal-scale consequences proceeds without a named, accountable human sign-off.
▾ outranks
2
People
The humans actually affected — the practitioner asking, the customer downstream, the public. Enforced through terminology checks that catch exclusionary or harmful language, and timing rules that stop an AI from going silent, acting too early, or overstaying its scope.
▾ outranks
3
Profit
Only after the first two are honored does the system optimize for efficiency and return — shown up in the pipeline as resilience (constraints that make sure the system lasts) and reinvestment (failures that turn into structural fixes, not repeated losses).

In other words: Profit is real and tracked, but it never buys its way around a Planet or People constraint. That ordering is architecture here, not a slogan — it's built into which checks run first and which flags halt the pipeline outright.

04

How it plugs into HSGA 4.0

The Holonic Semantic Governance Architecture (HSGA) is the organization's master rulebook — written once, at the enterprise level, defining what "safe," "compliant," "on-brand," and "in line with our values" actually mean, in terms specific enough that a machine can check against them. "Holonic" means it's built the same way at every scale: a single interaction, a team, a business unit, and the whole enterprise each have their own self-contained version of the same governance pattern, nested inside one another.

The RE Gateway is where HSGA 4.0 stops being a document and starts being enforced. It's the runtime layer: it takes the definitions and constraints written at the HSGA level and turns them into live, per-interaction checks — so a policy someone approved at the top of the organization doesn't just sit in a binder, it actually gates the next AI-assisted action taken in the business's name, the same way, every time, regardless of which team or system is asking.

HSGA 4.0 — defines

The enterprise rulebook

Standards, ontologies, and policy written once at the top: what "good" means, organization-wide, expressed precisely enough to be machine-checkable rather than left to interpretation.

RE Gateway — enforces

The runtime checkpoint

Takes HSGA's definitions and applies them live, interaction by interaction — the point where enterprise policy either actually governs behavior, or doesn't.

05

The front end of the Change4 investment pipeline

Change4 is where the organization decides what to fund: which change initiatives get prioritized, in what order, with what urgency. The question that decides all of that is simple to ask and usually hard to answer well — where is the organization actually falling short of what its people need, right now, and how do we know?

The RE Gateway is the front end of that supply chain because it's where the answer first becomes visible. Every interaction the Gateway governs produces structured evidence — not opinions: a requirement with no control in place yet, a specific kind of failure recurring often enough to trip an automatic review, a gap between what a stakeholder actually needed and what a system currently delivers. That evidence flows upstream into Change4 as a prioritized, sourced backlog of real operational need, so investment decisions are made against what's actually breaking and for whom — not against whoever argued loudest in the last planning meeting.

Live interaction→ Governance gap / finding→ Evidenced change need→ Change4 investment decision
06

End-to-end traceability & explainability for causality

Every output the Gateway governs carries a chain back to the specific requirement or stakeholder need that shaped it. Not "the AI said X" — but "the AI said X because Requirement R, which exists to serve Stakeholder Need N, required it, and it passed Check C to get here." That chain is preserved for every response, every gap, and every irreversible action, from the moment a request arrives to the moment it's delivered.

That's what makes the pipeline explainable rather than just logged. A log tells you what happened. A causal chain tells you why — which specific rule fired, which requirement it traced to, and which check it either passed or failed — so when something needs to be defended to a regulator, debugged after an incident, or simply understood by someone who wasn't in the room, the organization can point to the exact link in the chain instead of reconstructing a guess.

07

Governance and risk, integrated dynamically

The rules the Gateway enforces aren't a static policy binder reviewed once a year. They're live and queryable — evaluated fresh, for the specific request in front of it, at the moment it happens — including a real-time read on how risky and how reversible the proposed action actually is. That's what lets the pipeline guide a decision in the moment, rather than only judge it afterward.

Reversible

Can be undone cleanly. Proceeds under standard checks — no hold required.

Conditionally reversible

Proceeds, but within a defined undo window — the accountable person is notified and can still reverse it.

Irreversible

Cannot proceed without a named human's verified, logged authorization first. No exceptions, no override.

And because every check is evaluated in real time against current evidence, the same mechanism that governs a single interaction also watches for patterns across many of them — so when the same kind of failure keeps recurring, that's not left to be noticed eventually. It automatically triggers a formal review, which is also what feeds the evidenced case for change described above.

01

Six zones, one control structure

The console groups its eleven pipeline stages into six architectural zones. Each zone is a distinct trust boundary: requests only move forward, never around, and every zone hands off a specific, typed artifact to the next.

Zone 1

Elicitation Interface

Prompt-engineering layer. Personas issue a Structured Prompt Object (SPO) — identity, intent, domain, and scenario as typed fields, not free text.

Zone 2

Semantic Gateway

Requirement Context Bundle assembly, PEP authorization, and Compiled AI Directive compilation. The Gateway's own control logic lives here.

Zone 3

Ontology & Rules Store

Entity resolution, SHACL constraint evaluation, and SWRL rule execution — the constraint-enforcement engine the Gateway queries against.

Zone 4

Service Facade

The ten-criterion AcceptanceCriteria pipeline. Validates agent output before delivery and closes the STAMP feedback loop.

Zone 5

AI Agents + Vector DBs

The controlled process. Executes under CAD directive; retrieval is governed by the RDQE; behavior is watched by the UCA monitoring profile.

Zone 6

Simplex Action Gateway

The actuator boundary to the physical/external world. No Irreversible action executes without a verified human handshake.

UCA framework

Four unsafe control action types

Missing — no control action when one was required. Hazardous — a control action issued that creates a hazard. MisTimed — issued too early, too late, or out of sequence. WrongDuration — held too briefly or too long, i.e. scope exceeded. Each has a defined safe-state response, injected into the CAD as TMP (temporal monitor) and UCA rule activations at Step 3.

Defense in depth

The same hazard class, checked twice

UCA/TMP rules fire at compile time (Step 3–4) as the first layer. Zone 5's runtime monitors and Zone 4's AcceptanceCriteria form a second, independent layer at execution and delivery — so one missed check doesn't silently become a delivered failure.

03

The knowledge & constraint substrate

Every check the Gateway runs is a live query against a triplestore, not a lookup in a document. That's what makes "the rulebook" something a pipeline can actually enforce rather than merely reference.

Requirement individuals

Requirements as queryable objects

PersonaRequirements, JobRequirements, ComplianceRequirements, ControlRequirements, and ScenarioRequirements are first-class individuals in the triplestore — resolved fresh into the RCB for every interaction, not compiled once and left stale.

SHACL shapes

Structural constraint validation

A dynamically-assembled shape set, scoped to this persona/job/domain, evaluated against the annotated entity map. Must-priority failures halt compilation; Should-priority failures produce governance warnings.

SWRL rule families

REQ · TMP · UCA

REQ rules (8) translate requirement class membership into behavior. TMP rules (6, RDSG v2.0) govern time-based failure modes. UCA rules (5, RDSG v2.0) encode STPA unsafe-control-action constraints directly as runtime enforcement logic.

Hazard & safe-state individuals

UCARecord · SafeStateDefinition

Every FunctionalJob carries a full UCA profile — four hazard records with timeouts and safe-state actions — pre-fetched into the RCB's Safe-State Index so a mid-pipeline failure resolves instantly, without another triplestore round-trip.

06

Reversibility architecture

Zone 6 implements the Simplex Architecture principle directly: once an irreversible action executes, post-action feedback is useless, so verification has to happen before dispatch, not after.

Classify→ Flag (HANDSHAKE_REQUIRED)→ Authorize (Approval Token)→ Validate token→ Execute + immutable audit
Reversible

No handshake required. Standard SHACL/SWRL checks apply.

Conditionally reversible

ALLOW_WITH_CONDITIONS: proceeds inside a defined undo-window TTL, with the owner notified.

Irreversible

HANDSHAKE_REQUIRED. Compilation halts until a valid, scoped, fresh Approval Token is presented.

Two independent layers enforce this: the Step 4 Reversibility PEP catches it at compile time, before the CAD is even assembled. UCA-005 is the runtime backstop — if an Irreversible Zone 6 call somehow arrives without a HANDSHAKE_REQUIRED flag, it fires unconditionally and raises a control-structure-failure alert. An unowned Irreversible ControlRequirement (no valid approver role) halts CAD compilation outright — an unowned irreversible action cannot be governed, so it isn't allowed to exist.
