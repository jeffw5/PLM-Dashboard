Agent clearance tiers

Ties to the KE classification baseline

An agent's clearance is the highest sensitivity it may read. It follows the DomainToOperations rule: a process takes the highest level of the data it reads, so an agent's tier is set by its tools and data sources, not by its job title. Why a PEO can run this

Read-out

Assets that carry over

  • Co-employment is already a custody split. The client service agreement and its responsibility allocation (pes:ResponsibilityAllocation) are the template for LIA.01.
  • Pooled risk. A master health plan and pooled workers' comp are the model for a master Agent Comp policy across many small clients.
  • Certification habits. CPEO and ESAC accreditation are the precedent for CMP.01; the AEO certifies the base agent the way the PEO certifies its payroll tax handling.
  • Classification already built. The C0–C4 levels, regime floors and the high-water-mark rule give clearance tiers a governed source.

Where the PEO has to build new

  • Machine identity and key operations (IDN.01–02) are a security-engineering function no PEO runs today.
  • Actuarial data. Carriers price workers' comp on decades of claims. Agent loss history barely exists, so AGC lines start as a partner product.
  • Real-time enforcement. A 250 ms circuit breaker is an operations platform, not a service team.
  • Six services have no PEO analog; they are flagged new in the catalog.

Validate before using

  • "Synthetic respondeat superior" is a contract construct. No court has applied vicarious-liability doctrine to an agent this way; counsel should review the allocation schedule.
  • The insurance lines assume a carrier will write them. Confirm appetite before putting them in the core bundle.
  • AI regulations named in CMP.02–03 are examples; effective dates and scope change often and must be checked per jurisdiction.
  • Service IDs are stable and each maps to its PEO analog with skos:closeMatch in the SKOS export.
