Same year, two workforces

Human reserve, January to December

Extractive Utilization 95–100%

Capacity is drawn down for quarterly output and restored by turnover. Reserves end the year lower; the cost shows up as recruiting, ramp time and claims.

Regenerative Utilization capped at 80%

Twenty percent of capacity goes to development, mentoring and recovery. Load still varies, but people come back from peaks and the year ends with more reserve.

Illustrations of the pattern, not measured data. The scorecard below defines how a PEO would measure it.

Enterprise lab protocol

PEO-as-sandbox · LAB services in order

  1. Stand up the entity

    A new subsidiary co-employed by the PEO, with its own handbook, review cycle and pay design (LAB.01). Check controlled-group effects first.

  2. Pre-register the test

    State the hypothesis, the practice under test (pods, four-day week, profit loop), duration of at least 12 months and the scorecard measures that decide it (LAB.02).

  3. Pick a comparison group

    A matched cohort of similar SMB clients from the PEO pool, by industry, size and region, reported only as an aggregate (LAB.04).

  4. Run and report quarterly

    Scorecard results for the lab against its comparison group, with person-level data kept inside the PEO.

  5. Decide what scales

    A scale-back playbook names which policies transfer to the parent, what harmonization costs, and what stays in the lab (LAB.05).

Read-out

What the map says

Why a PEO is the right host

  • Pooling is the business. Sabbatical funds, parental leave pools and dependent-care subsidies are too expensive for a 40-person firm alone and cheap per head across a large co-employed base.
  • The data already flows through it. Time, leave, turnover and promotion data sit in the PEO's systems, so the scorecard is a reporting product, not a new data collection.
  • Cross-client redeployment is unique to the model. No single SMB can run a talent exchange. A PEO with thousands of clients can.
  • Work-share filing is a quick win. Many states run short-time compensation programs that partially replace wages when hours are cut instead of jobs. A PEO already files unemployment claims.

Where the brief needs correcting

  • A carve-out does not escape controlled-group rules. A subsidiary stays in the parent's controlled group for benefit nondiscrimination testing and ACA employer status, and its plans are still ERISA plans. What it does escape is the handbook, review cycle and pay design.
  • Drop biometric tracking. Biometrics are C4 under Illinois BIPA, and wellness data is limited by ADA and GINA. The scorecard uses voluntary surveys and aggregates instead.
  • The 80% cap is on assigned work, not pay. Reserved development and recovery time is still paid working time for non-exempt staff.
  • Secondments raise joint-employer and workers' comp questions, and a pooled furlough fund may count as insurance. Both need structuring with counsel.

Validate before using

  • Readiness ratings assume HR360 capabilities from the PEO Service Map; check them against the internal product catalog.
  • Partner examples (Lyra, Modern Health) are illustrative, not commitments.
  • Service IDs are stable; the SKOS export links each one to its PEO base service with skos:closeMatch.
