Find the employment laws that apply to any client worksite, and who carries each one

The PEO Standards & Compliance Map shows, for any client worksite, which federal, state and local employment laws apply at its headcount, what each law requires, and whether the PEO or the client carries it. It runs in a web browser from one file and needs no install.

The browser page reads all six files at once, so every view shows the same facts. Governance rules check each layer before a release.

One question — which laws apply at a client worksite — takes about five minutes the first time.

  1. Open PEO Standards & Compliance Map.html in any browser. It needs an internet connection the first time to load its drawing library.
  2. Pick a view on the left. Jurisdiction map shows every law by level and where it applies; Co-employment shows the PEO, clients, worksites and who carries what.
  3. Hover any box to read its attributes and a plain-language explanation. Click to pin it in the right-hand panel with every statement about it. Double-click to show only its neighbours.
  4. Run the Applicability check: choose a worksite (or a state or city), enter the client's headcount, press Check. The map lists what applies, what is below the headcount threshold, what a local law displaces, and who is responsible for each requirement.
  5. Switch layout above the graph: Network for relationships, Cluster to group by level, topic or status, Hierarchy for nation → state → county → city, List for a sortable table.

The Path finder traces how any two items connect — for example, a worksite to the ISO standard that governs how its data is managed.

  • Exposure by market. Choose Cluster layout, group by Level of government. Local rules cluster in Chicago, Cook County and New York City; Texas bars most local employment rules.
  • Exposure by client. Run the applicability check. The fictional Chicago bakery with 38 employees is subject to 21 standards: 13 federal, 5 state and 3 local. The ACA employer mandate, FMLA, WARN and Chicago Fair Workweek start at 50 or 100 employees, so growth changes the obligation set.
  • The co-employment split. Its client service agreement assigns 8 requirements: 1 to the PEO, 3 shared and 4 to the client. Ten of the 17 requirements that apply at that site have no clause yet — a contract and pricing question, not only a compliance one.
  • Entering a market. Pick a state in the applicability check before signing a client there. Texas, for example, requires a PEO license for even one assigned employee.

  • Every standard carries its citation, enforcing agency, effective date, headcount threshold and legal status. Pin a law to see them, plus the requirements it creates.

Precedence is explicit. Four relationships say how laws interact:

preempted byThe lower law has no effect. The Austin paid sick time ordinance is held preempted by the Texas Minimum Wage Act.
displaced byThe law yields where another applies. The Illinois Paid Leave for All Workers Act and the Cook County ordinance yield to Chicago's paid leave ordinance for Chicago workers.
supplemented byBoth apply and the more protective governs. Chicago's $17.05 minimum wage supplements the Illinois and Cook County rates.
amendsIllinois HB 3773 adds AI-in-employment duties to the Illinois Human Rights Act from January 1, 2026.
  • History is kept, not overwritten. The 2024 federal overtime rule stays in the map marked Rescinded. Its status note records the court vacatur and DOL's rescission effective May 15, 2026, which restored the $684-per-week threshold.
  • Check verification before citing. Each record shows Checked with a date and source link, or Not yet verified. Use Filter → Laws: only those not yet verified to see the review queue.

The map is a model of the law, not legal advice. Counsel should review the rules for co-employment headcount counting, which vary by statute.

Use the applicability check as the working tool: it turns a location and a headcount into a list of duties, with an owner for each.

  1. Choose the client's worksite and confirm its headcount.
  2. Read the five result groups: Applies, Below headcount threshold, Displaced by a local ordinance, Not in force here, and PEO obligations such as CPEO tax certification.
  3. Read the requirements list. Each row names the responsible party from the client service agreement, or no clause yet.
  4. Press Show only these to see just the applicable laws, their jurisdictions and enforcing agencies. Switch to List layout to sort and scan them.
Worked example
A 12-person design studio in Brooklyn. Title VII, the ADA and six other federal laws do not reach it, but the New York State Human Rights Law covers every employer. NYC Local Law 144 applies if it screens candidates with an automated tool. Its agreement assigns the annual bias audit to the PEO, which runs the screening tool, and shares the candidate notice.

The Requirements view groups every duty by compliance topic. Use it to build control checklists by topic rather than by statute.

Use the map to find exposure that a law-by-law review misses: thresholds about to be crossed, laws in flux, new AI duties, and records nobody has verified.

  • Threshold proximity. Change the headcount in the applicability check and rerun it. Laws move from Below headcount threshold to Applies at 15, 20, 50 and 100 employees.
  • Laws in flux. Filter → Legal status separates in-force, challenged, preempted and rescinded laws. Texas HB 2127, which bars most local employment rules, is in force but still under legal challenge.
  • AI in employment decisions. The Worker privacy and AI topic links Illinois HB 3773 and NYC Local Law 144 to their notice, bias-audit and non-discrimination duties. Pair it with the ISO/IEC 42005 impact-assessment standard in the governance view.
  • Data you can trust. 15 of the 47 standards have been checked against a published source; 32 have not. The quality rules (peo-shapes.ttl) list the gap as warnings, so it is visible rather than hidden.
  • Unassigned duties. A requirement with no clause yet in the agreement is an allocation risk between the PEO and the client.

The map is a working prototype of a governed knowledge graph: one OWL ontology, one SKOS taxonomy, reference data, client data and quality rules, read directly by the browser page.

  • Reasoning. The ontology infers which standards a worksite is subject to from its jurisdiction chain, and classifies each law as federal, state or local.
  • Governance. The Governance lifecycle view, best in Hierarchy layout, shows each stage from scoping to retirement and the ISO standards that guide it. No single ISO standard covers the whole lifecycle; ISO/IEC 5392:2024 is the umbrella, and 24 of the 40 standards are new or revised since 2022.
  • Deployment. Persist the slow-changing reference data in a graph store (RDF, or a property graph queried with ISO GQL). Expose client, worksite and headcount data from the HR system as a virtual graph (SQL/PGQ or R2RML) so it is never copied or stale.
  • Change control. Edit the Turtle files, run the quality rules, then rebuild the page, which embeds the files.

Treat the map as a prototype for decisions about scope and investment, not as a compliance system of record.

  • Not legal advice. It models the law; counsel confirms how each rule applies.
  • Sample scope. Federal law plus Illinois, Texas, California and New York, and Chicago, Cook County, New York City and Austin. California cities are not modeled.
  • Partly verified. 15 of 47 standards were checked against a source on September 30, 2026. Clear the other 32 before any production use.
  • Simplified thresholds. The check compares headcount with each law's minimum. Real counting rules (employees within 75 miles for FMLA, full-time equivalents for the ACA, co-employed headcount) are noted per law but not calculated.
  • Fictional client data. The PEO, clients and agreements are invented to show the method.
  • Point in time. Wage rates and statuses are dated records; the page does not update itself.
