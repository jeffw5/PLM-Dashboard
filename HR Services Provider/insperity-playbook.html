How I got ready for the Insperity interview

I've got a conversation coming up with a company called Insperity — and I wanted to walk you through what I actually did to prepare, and what all these files I've been buried in for the past couple weeks are.

Insperity handles HR for other businesses — payroll, benefits, hiring paperwork, staying on the right side of employment law — for over 300,000 employees across thousands of client companies. My conversation is likely with David Burnley, who leads product innovation there.

The short version

1

Learn who I'm talking to, and what they're dealing with

Before anything else, I researched David and the company — their size, their recent projects, and the kinds of problems a company their size runs into.

2

Figure out where I could actually help

I looked at what Insperity has said publicly about the problems they're trying to solve, and matched those up against the kind of work I've spent my career doing.

3

Don't just say it — build it

Instead of walking in with a resume and a few talking points, I built working prototypes that show how I'd approach their problems. It's the difference between telling someone you can cook and actually handing them a plate.

What each thing actually is

Interview Prep Sheet PDF

My cheat sheet on the person and the company.

Notes on David Burnley's background, what Insperity does and how big it is, and a handful of sharp questions I plan to ask. This is the "know who you're talking to" homework.

Insperity Impact PDF

The pitch — here's where I think I can help.

A short proposal laying out problems Insperity likely has — things like keeping up with different labor laws in every state, connecting HR systems that don't talk to each other, and making sure an AI assistant doesn't confidently give a wrong answer about someone's benefits — and how my approach could fix each one.

IIH Interactive Interactive demo

A working sketch of a smarter, safer HR chatbot.

Insperity is building an AI assistant for employees. This is my concept for how to make it trustworthy — every answer can be traced back to the actual policy it came from, and it can't take a risky action (like changing someone's pay) without a human checking first. Also includes a step-by-step plan for rolling it out safely over about a year and a half.

HR Services Business Model Interactive map

A map of how Insperity's business actually fits together.

A clickable diagram of Insperity's services and how they connect — payroll, benefits, compliance, hiring, and so on. I built this to prove I understand the business itself, not just the technology underneath it.

Accelerator Console Interactive demo

Six tools I've built, laid out as a menu.

A dashboard showing six ideas I've developed over the years — things like a system for writing a rule once and having it enforced everywhere automatically, and a "circuit breaker" that pauses an AI assistant if it starts guessing instead of knowing. Each one is mapped directly to something Insperity has said they care about.

Compliance SSOT Dashboard Prototype dashboard

One place to keep every employment rule straight.

"SSOT" just means "single source of truth" — one place that's always right, instead of five spreadsheets that disagree. This prototype shows how a company could track every federal, state, and local employment rule in one system, so nothing slips through when a law changes.

Why go to all this trouble

Anyone can say "I understand your business" in an interview. Showing up with working examples that actually reflect their specific problems is a much harder thing to fake — and it's a much easier thing to remember. That's the whole strategy: less talking about the work, more showing it.

Wish me luck.
