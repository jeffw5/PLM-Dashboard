Executive vision

Compass sets direction. Copilot does the work. The Keel keeps both true.

Vision statement Make Insperity the PEO whose AI can show its work. Compass turns strategy, law and risk appetite into policy that machines enforce. A stack-independent semantic Keel gives every platform, from Workday to Microsoft 365, one governed meaning for every worker, client, rule and plan. Copilot speeds up every service moment for worksite employees, client leaders and Insperity's own teams, and every answer and action traces back to the rule and the statute behind it.

Insperity already runs AI agents in HR360 and delivers HRScale on Workday for mid-market clients, and its leadership has said AI does not replace experienced human judgment. This design builds on both: governed agents across every platform, with a person holding every irreversible action.

Stack independence and interoperability

The Keel owns contracts, not products. Canonical knowledge lives in W3C and open standards, and every vendor surface receives a generated projection that carries the source URI. That lets Insperity run on Microsoft today, keep Workday as the system of record for HRScale clients, and change model or cloud providers later without rewriting the knowledge.

ADR-01

Canonical truth lives in open standards

OWL, SKOS, SHACL and LKIF are the source. Fabric IQ ontologies, Workday business-process conditions, Purview glossary terms and dbt tests are generated from it.

Consequence: nobody hand-edits a projection. The mediation holon detects drift and flags it in CI.

ADR-02

Enforcement sits in the request path, outside any model vendor

Every agent, whatever vendor built it, reaches data and actions through the gateway's policy enforcement point.

Consequence: changing the LLM or the copilot product changes nothing about what is allowed.

ADR-03

Every agent is an identity with a governed profile

Agents get a directory identity and a profile listing allowed tools, tenants and autonomy level, just as employees get personal models.

Consequence: agent sprawl becomes visible and auditable. Unregistered agents cannot reach Keel tools.

ADR-04

Replaceability is tested, not claimed

Any adapter can be replaced within one quarter without changing L1 or L2 holons. One swap is exercised at every phase gate.

Consequence: lock-in risk is measured and reported to the ARB each phase.

Decisions needed and risks

Decisions that set Phase 0 scope

  • Microsoft licensing in place. Whether Purview, Fabric capacity and Copilot Studio are already licensed determines which adapters come first.
  • System of record by client segment. HRScale clients on Workday and others on Insperity's platform sets the adapter order and the parity scope.
  • Model hosting. Which model providers are approved for PHI-adjacent content, and where they run.
  • Council membership. Legal, compliance, domain owners and IT architecture, with the authority to approve rule changes.

Top risks and mitigations

  • Benefits answers are high stakes. MTBH thresholds, circuit-breaker escalation to people, and Simplex holds on anything irreversible.
  • State AI and hiring laws keep changing. The regulatory change discovery holon and AEDT audits treat them as rules like any other.
  • Service teams may not adopt the tools. Start with time-back uses in Observe mode, and let teams see their own metrics.
  • Semantics become a bottleneck. ORSD templates, familiar authoring tools, and a council with a published cycle-time target.
