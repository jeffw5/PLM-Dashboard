Holonic Semantic Governance Architecture

HSGA

v4.2 · Jeffrey Wallk, The Value Enablement Group, LLC · self-published draft, uncertified

HSGA is an enterprise reference architecture for governing what knowledge and AI systems mean, what they may be used for, and what actions they may authorize — at both design time and run time. It uses Arthur Koestler's holon concept (an entity that is simultaneously a self-contained whole and a composable part) as its organizing grammar, synthesizing nine prior internal frameworks into one architecture. It is process-first: the Process Step is the elementary, operand-agnostic unit, wrapped in a governance contract — identity, SHACL invariant, ORSD/KGCL lifecycle — that composes upward through Service, Value Stream, Supply Chain, and Value Chain regardless of what is flowing through it. Critically, HSGA is not just narrative description: a live, versioned OWL/SHACL ontology suite built at Verizon instantiates its classes and constraints as executable, queryable structure.

Defining elements

  • Four vertical holonic levels (L1 Knowledge → L4 Enterprise Holarchy) plus a lateral Mediation Holon bridging top-down ontology to bottom-up implementation
  • Circuit Breaker / MTBH — dimensional health monitoring that detects and contains AI hallucination before it can poison training data
  • LKIF deontic norms — obligation, permission, prohibition, violation, sanction — attached to every holon
  • Decision Model & Measurement — a decision, not a requirement, is the first-class object mediating problem and solution
  • Grounded in Basic Formal Ontology (BFO), an ISO/IEC 21838-2 top-level ontology

Hetero-Functional Graph Theory

HFGT

Farid, Hosseini & Little (2026) · Systems Engineering (Wiley), DOI 10.1002/sys.70056 · peer-reviewed, NSF-funded

HFGT is a mathematical formalism for modeling systems-of-systems, built as a methodological bridge between model-based systems engineering — qualitative, heterogeneous, but hard to compute over — and network science, which offers rigorous matrix-based analysis but has historically flattened that heterogeneity into uniform nodes and edges. It is operand-first: its foundational taxonomy (Table 1) crosses five generic functions — Transform, Transport, Store, Exchange, Control — against five universal substances — Living Organisms, Matter, Energy, Information, Money — and that operand typing is load-bearing, giving the framework's mathematics physical meaning (conservation of matter and energy, non-rivalry of information, fungibility of money) for free. The core grammar — a resource executes a process on an operand — compresses into an operand net per operand, then third-order incidence tensors, then a hetero-functional adjacency matrix, from which structural-robustness metrics can be computed directly rather than argued. The work traces to 2006–2008 and has been applied, in published and cited studies, to power grids, potable water, transportation, mass-customized production, healthcare delivery, and megaproject engineering.

Defining elements

  • Three meta-architecture tiers (Instantiated → Reference → Meta-Architecture), organized by generalization depth
  • Ullman's triangle plus four linguistic properties — soundness, completeness, lucidity, laconicity — used to test whether a model is ontologically adequate
  • Operand net (Petri net) per operand, compressed into third-order hetero-functional incidence tensors
  • Hetero-functional adjacency matrix — a computed structural-robustness metric, not an asserted one
  • Resources typed by capability: Transformation Resource, Independent Buffer, Transportation Resource
