SHEET 00 — BUSINESS SUMMARY

Decision Model & Measurement

A logical architecture for making every decision traceable, federated across the organizations it touches, measurable against what actually happens — and answerable, in strict order, to the planet, the people, and only then the balance sheet.

Most organizations decide the way they always have: informally, locally, and without a record of what was chosen or why. A decision made inside one team's process quietly reaches into a partner's supply chain, a regulator's rulebook, or a customer's daily life — and nobody traces that reach until something breaks.

This platform treats every decision as a first-class, decomposable object — a Decision Breakdown Structure (DBS) — with its own class, governance status, criteria, and candidate alternatives. Any node opens into its own DBS, recursively. Those structures don't stop at a team's boundary: a Graph of Graphs federates five organizational strata — Intra-Domain through Ecosystem — so a decision's real reach is visible, not assumed.

Two disciplines close the loop. A Causal Impact Map forecasts how each alternative ripples into strategy, services, processes, and assets before anything is committed. What gets measured afterward — inside a digital twin or the real operation — is compared back against that prediction, and the difference is what makes the next decision better than this one.

SHEET 03

Digital Twins

Decision-making and measurement aren't bolted onto the twin from outside — they live inside it. The decision layer sits at the twin's core; instrumentation is woven through its surface.

A digital twin is only useful to a decision architecture if it's addressable by the same structure that makes the decision — otherwise it's a dashboard, not a sandbox. Here, the Decision Layer (the DBS plus its Causal Impact Map) doesn't sit beside the twin — it sits at the center of it, as the twin's own decision-making core. "What happens to fulfillment throughput if we choose parallel intake instead of linear sequential flow" is answerable against the twin's live state, from inside the loop, not argued about in a room outside it.

Measurement is instrumented the same way — not a downstream report, but sensors woven through the twin's surface, continuously comparing simulated state to real-world telemetry. That's what makes the twin immersive rather than illustrative: a person or an agent working inside it is deciding, simulating, and measuring in the same continuous space, not switching between three disconnected tools.

Federation from Sheet 02 still applies at every layer: a decision inside one domain's twin can reach an outer stratum's twin through the same dashed edges — a Process Architecture change simulated at Intra-Domain can be checked against its downstream effect on a Value Chain twin before it ships.

SHEET 04

Guardrails: Planet › People › Profit

Planet, People, Profit is not a values statement hung on a wall — it's a decision gate, evaluated in that order, every time. An alternative that degrades a planetary boundary doesn't advance to the People gate no matter how it scores there; an alternative that clears Planet but degrades safety, dignity, or equity doesn't advance to Profit no matter how much cheaper it is.

This is a lexicographic ordering, not a weighted score. A weighted score lets a large enough Profit number buy back a Planet or People failure; a lexicographic gate structurally cannot. Of three candidate alternatives entering the funnel below, watch the count narrow at each gate — that's the mechanism, not a metaphor.

A rejection at Gate 1 or Gate 2 isn't a dead end — both routes converge into a single redesign task that re-enters at the top as a new candidate alternative, not a closed door.

PLANET — first, non-negotiable PEOPLE — second, non-negotiable PROFIT — optimized last, within what remains
SHEET 05

Causal Analysis → Measurement → Better Decisions

Causal analysis and measurement are two ends of the same loop, and neither means anything without the other. The Causal Impact Map is a prediction — what should happen to strategy, services, processes, and assets if this alternative is chosen. Measurement is what actually happened, captured from the digital twin or the operation itself. The gap between the two is the signal.

When the gap is small, the model behind the prediction gets more trustworthy, and the next decision in the same category can lean on it with more confidence — the same logic the platform already applies to RSM surrogate strategies and Pareto frontiers in engineering decisions, generalized to every category of decision. When the gap is large, the causal assumption itself gets revised.

This is what "optimize decision making for people and machines" means in practice: a person choosing an alternative and an AI agent proposing one draw on the same measured history, checked against the same guardrail order, and both improve at the same rate the loop turns. The loop is the optimizer.

Inter-Domain Frame

Domains sense change both intra-domain (within their own boundary) and inter-domain (horizontal peers, vertical strategy/execution flow). Edges below represent dependency & impact, not just data flow.

Business Operation Frame

This is where design-time decisions meet run-time reality: the recurring operational loop (Identify Defects → Find Cause → Choose Fix → Plan/Protect Fix) that keeps the value stream and supply chain running day to day.

Value Chain Frame

Once decisions cross a company boundary, the receiving company's own domain graph (Step 1) is opaque to you by default — you only see the interface: what was decided, what's expected in return.

Ecosystem Frame

3rd parties are heterogeneous: individuals, automated agents, other companies, and institutions each decide under different authority and incentive structures. Model each as its own decision-capable node.
